# Show the second screen as a passive Presentation, and lay Loom out by its window's size

The AYN Thor has two screens: the top one, 1920×1080 at 369 dpi (832×468 dp with the bars hidden), where Cocoon opens Loom, and a bottom one, 1240×1080 (538 dp wide), which Cocoon fills with its own bottom screen. Loom 0.3 drew one layout for the top screen and nothing below it. The owner's choice (2026-10-15) reverses the roadmap's draft, which had the details on top and the list below: the list and all input stay on the top screen, and the bottom screen shows a passive panel of what the pad is on, as long as Loom is in front. The same release had to fit any screen Loom could be opened on (the bottom screen itself, phones, tablets) without being designed twice.

**The panel is an `android.app.Presentation`**, a window of Loom's own activity on the other display, not a second activity. Its display is picked from `DISPLAY_CATEGORY_PRESENTATION` by its flags, since a display's type is hidden from apps: a presentation display that isn't Loom's own, isn't private, is secure (a screen recording or an app sharing the screen can't make a secure display without a system permission) and isn't off; of those, the last in the system's order, which puts the built-in bottom screen ahead of a monitor. Its window is `FLAG_NOT_FOCUSABLE | FLAG_NOT_TOUCH_MODAL`, so it never takes the pad's focus, and it stays touchable on purpose: a tap that fell through would land on Cocoon's bottom screen, and AYN's Auto-lock gives the pad to the screen last touched. A tap on the panel does nothing but count as using Loom. Its Compose content is created in the presentation's own context, so it measures the bottom screen (538×444 dp there), and it shares the activity's lifecycle and saved-state owners but not its `ViewModelStoreOwner`: a ViewModel asked for there would be a second copy, so the screens on top publish what the panel shows (`CompanionContent`, through `CompanionHolder`) and it only draws that. What it shows: the library's focused game (its cover, title, state, save, and what RomM says of it: the year, genres, companies and summary), the queue's focused download or save conflict with both sides, the pairing QR code during setup, and a strip of what the queue holds.

**When it goes.** `SecondScreenController` shows it only while Loom is started and the top resumed activity, the second screen is on and isn't Loom's own, and **Use the second screen** (Settings → Controls & screens) is on. So it goes at once on Home or a game launch (Loom stops), when another app is resumed on the other screen (Loom stops being top resumed, though it keeps window focus there), and when the bottom screen turns off or goes, and comes back as each of these undoes. AYN's Focus Mode is read from `Settings.System` `screen_focus_lock` and watched: with the pad locked to the bottom screen (2) while Loom is on top, the panel goes, because the keys go to whatever is under it, and Loom says so in place of its button hints; with the pad locked to the top (1) while Loom is on the bottom screen, it says that instead. The bottom screen goes all black after 3 minutes without a button or a touch reaching Loom, as an OLED panel keeps the mark of what it shows for long; a download moving on doesn't keep it lit, a pairing code does.

**R3 hides it** (an owner decision made after the plan). Because the panel is touchable and covers the bottom screen while Loom is in front, an app the owner opened there (a guide, a video) can't be reached short of leaving Loom or turning the switch off. `KEYCODE_BUTTON_THUMBR` (the Thor's Odin Controller sends `BTN_THUMBR`) now toggles the panel. It is taken at `Activity.dispatchKeyEvent` (`GamepadKeys.dispatch`), before Compose, any dialog and Android's fallback key for an unused pad button, so it works on every screen and in dialogs and nothing else ever acts on it. The state lives in `SecondScreenController` and isn't persisted. Only `onStart` clears it, so using that app and coming back to Loom (top resumed again), a configuration change and a display change keep the panel hidden, while Home or a game launch and coming back bring it back. R3 does nothing when no panel would show (no second screen, the switch off, Loom on the bottom screen, Focus Mode on the bottom screen), so it never hides one ahead of time. Taps on the panel still do nothing. The hint bar doesn't mention R3; the switch's explanation in Settings and the README's Controls do. The trade-off stays: while Loom is in front with the panel shown, it covers whatever is open on the bottom screen, as Loom can't tell an app is there.

**Layout by the window's size.** Every screen reads its window's size from `LocalWindowInfo.current.containerDpSize` and sorts it by Material's width breakpoints, 600 and 840 dp, with 480 dp as tall enough (`loomLayout`). `LocalConfiguration` describes the display, not the window, and Compose's lint (`ConfigurationScreenWidthHeight`, an error here) refuses it. The Thor's top screen, 832 dp, is MEDIUM, the layout every screen was designed for. COMPACT (the bottom screen, a phone in portrait) names the platform in the header with a picker instead of the rail, cycles one filter chip, gives dialogs the full width with buttons that wrap, and stacks setup's columns. EXPANDED (a phone on its side, a tablet in landscape) shows the focused game's panel beside the games, unless a second screen already shows it. Settings has its own breakpoint, 800 dp: below it, its section list and an open section, each wanting most of the width, take the screen in turn. `configChanges` covers size, layout, density and orientation, so moving Loom between the screens, or turning a phone, keeps its place instead of recreating it. Content fits without scrolling on the Thor's screens, phones in portrait and tablets; on a phone on its side (about 411 dp tall) a dialog or a Settings section may scroll, by the owner's choice.

**What the panel needs from the catalog.** RomM's summary, release year (its `first_release_date` is in milliseconds, read as a UTC year), genres (three at most) and companies (two at most) are kept in their own table, `rom_details`, and read only for the focused game: the library's rows are read again at every download tick and would otherwise carry 20,000 summaries, each cut to about 1,000 characters at a word. Raising `CatalogSyncer.CATALOG_FIELDS` makes the first sync after the upgrade a full one, which Loom queues by itself on the first open. The panel's cover is made from RomM's large art, only for the game the focus has rested on for half a second, through the cover store's one fetch, so "Only on Wi-Fi" holds it back on a metered network; that fetch also makes the game's sharp thumbnail if it had none, and only the newest 48 are kept. Until it is there, the thumbnail is shown.

## Considered Options

- **The roadmap's draft: details on top, the list on the bottom screen.** The top screen is where Cocoon opens Loom, where the pad's focus is, and the one with room for the list; 538 dp would have made the list the compact one everywhere.
- **A second activity on the bottom display** (`ActivityOptions.setLaunchDisplayId`): it would be resumed and focusable on its own, compete with Loom for the pad and top-resumed state under AYN's focus rules, and outlive Loom unless closed by hand. A presentation is part of Loom's window set and needs none of that.
- **An untouchable panel** (`FLAG_NOT_TOUCHABLE`): a tap would go through to Cocoon's bottom screen and, under Auto-lock, take the pad from Loom.
- **Hiding on window-focus loss rather than top-resumed loss**: with per-display focus Loom keeps window focus while an app starts on the bottom screen, and the notification shade takes window focus, which would make the panel flicker.
- **`material3-adaptive` or `androidx.window` size classes**: the adaptive helper also starts tracking folding posture, which Loom doesn't need. Three constants over the window's size are enough.
- **For reaching an app on the bottom screen:** a tap on the panel (under Auto-lock the tap itself takes the pad from Loom); relying on the panel already going while that app is resumed (it comes back over the app as soon as Loom is turned back to, which is what the owner didn't want); and keeping the hide across opens (the owner wants a fresh open to show the panel again). The switch in Settings remains for turning the panel off for good.
- **Hero covers fetched on every focus move, or for every game**: the large art is about 0.5 MB, and running through the grid would fetch one per card; waiting half a second for the focus to rest, and keeping only the newest 48 (a few MB), bounds both.
